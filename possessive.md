# American English Homophone Reference — Possessive & Plural List

**1,396 homophone sets · 2,795 words · forms of one word**

A dictionary-format reference to homophones that differ only in their grammatical ending: a word against its own possessive, plural, third-person or comparative form — *engine's / engines*, *year's / years*, *act's / acts*. These are real homophones in speech. The companion file **corelist.md** holds the 1,282 sets that pair different words.

---

## How to read an entry

```
/ˈɛndʒənz/ — **engine's** *n.* (poss. of engine) motor that converts thermal energy to mechanical work · **engines** *n.* (pl. of engine) motor that converts thermal energy to mechanical work
```

* Entries are numbered in bold for cross-reference; the example above omits the number.
* The transcription between slashes is a General American phonemic transcription (IPA), with primary stress marked ˈ and secondary stress ˌ.
* The parenthetical note after each gloss states which form the headword is: *(poss. of engine)*, *(pl. of engine)*, *(3sg of act)*, *(past of eat)*.
* The appendix continues the numbering with the prefix **A**.

## What counts here

* Every set here consists of forms of **one and the same word**, differing only by a possessive *-'s* or a plural, third-person or comparative ending. They are genuine homophones in speech, which is why they belong in a homophone reference; they are separated out because their members are not different words.
* **Only same-headword sets appear.** The companion file **corelist.md** holds the 1,282 sets of distinct words; together the two files are the 2,678 sets of the full analysis.
* **Contractions are not here.** *It's / its*, *let's / lets* and *we're / were* pair different words and therefore sit in corelist.md. Possessives written with *-'s* are forms of their base word, so they belong here rather than there — *engine's / engines* is the pattern.
* **Accent-dependent and reduced forms** follow the same pronunciation rule as in corelist.md.
* **Names and place names** follow the same appendix arrangement.

**Method.** Candidate pronunciations were taken from CMUdict (American English, 126,052 headwords) and grouped by identical phoneme strings with stress retained. Headwords were validated against a US English spell-checking dictionary to remove misspellings, and against WordNet to separate ordinary vocabulary from names. A set is retained when at least one member occurs in modern English prose at a rate of roughly one in three million words or better. Every set was reviewed by hand; false collisions produced by erroneous pronunciations in the source dictionary were removed. These sets are the residue of a further lemma pass: every headword was reduced to its dictionary lemma, and a set was placed here when all of its members resolve to one headword through a possessive clitic or a plural, third-person or comparative ending.

**Two files.** possessive.md (this file) and corelist.md are exact complements: 1,396 + 1,282 = 2,678 sets, with no set in both. Together they reproduce the full 2,678-set, 5,638-word analysis.
